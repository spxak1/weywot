∿ Resonance Curve

What It Shows
Plots RMS current against frequency for the series RLC circuit, revealing the resonance peak where current is maximum.
At resonance Xʟ = Xᴄ, so impedance is purely resistive (Z = R) and current reaches Imax = V / R.
The orange dashed line marks your current operating frequency. A pink dot shows the current operating point on the curve.
Key Parameters
f₀ — resonant frequency = 1 / (2π√LC), where current is maximum.
Q — quality factor = (1/R)√(L/C). Higher Q means a sharper, taller peak.
BW — bandwidth = f₀ / Q, the frequency range where current exceeds Imax / √2.
Circuit Requirements
Requires exactly one resistor (or bulb), one capacitor, and one inductor in a series loop. Wires between components are fine. Variable types (slider components) are supported.
The checkbox is greyed out if these conditions are not met.
Fixed Scales & Exploring
When you tick the checkbox, the axes lock based on the current R, L, and C values. This means as you drag component sliders you can watch the curve change in real time against a fixed reference:
• Increase R — peak drops (lower Imax) and broadens.
• Decrease R — peak rises and sharpens (higher Q).
• Change L or C — peak shifts left or right as f₀ changes.
To reset the scales to match new values, untick and re-tick the checkbox.
Manual Frequency Range
Use the f min and f max fields to set the graph's frequency axis manually. Accepts plain numbers in Hz, or with suffix: 1.5k = 1500 Hz, 2M = 2 MHz.
Leave a field empty (or press Auto) to use the auto-computed range. The greyed-out placeholder shows the auto value for reference.
Press Enter or click away to apply. Radio stations will automatically redistribute across the new range.
Graph Controls
Drag the title bar to reposition the graph.
Click the graph body to enlarge it full-size.
Click outside the enlarged graph to shrink it back.

📻 Radio Tuner Demo

How It Works
Five radio stations (A–E) broadcast at fixed frequencies across the resonance range. Each plays a different sound (sine, triangle, square, sawtooth, dual-tone) so you can hear which station you are tuned to.

Your RLC circuit acts as a tuning filter. Only stations near f₀ are heard loudly. The yellow tuning indicator on the graph shows where f₀ sits relative to the stations.
Controls
Variable Capacitor — sweeps f₀ across the band (the tuning dial).
Resistance — controls Q factor. Lower R = higher Q = sharper tuning.
Separation slider — how close together the stations are. Low = packed tight (needs high Q), high = spread out (easy to separate).
Volume slider — master audio volume.
Requirements
Needs a series RLC circuit with a signal generator (same as Resonance Curve).
Best with a variable capacitor so you can sweep through stations.

☰ Oscilloscope

What It Shows
A dual-channel oscilloscope displaying voltage vs time waveforms across circuit components.
CH1 (green) and CH2 (yellow) can each monitor a different component, letting you compare amplitudes and measure phase differences.
Assigning Channels
With the oscilloscope enabled, click any component (not wires or signal generators) to add it to the active channel.
• Click CH1 or CH2 label to switch which channel receives clicks
• You can select multiple adjacent components on each channel (like V-I Waveform) to see the combined voltage
• Click an assigned component again to remove it
A green (CH1) or yellow (CH2) badge appears on assigned components.
Measuring Voltage
Set V/div so the trace fills most of the screen. Count grid squares from the centre line to the peak. Multiply by V/div to get Vpk.
Then Vrms = Vpk / √2.
Measuring Period & Frequency
Set time/div so you can see 1–2 complete cycles. Count grid squares for one full cycle (peak to next peak). Multiply by time/div to get period T.
Then f = 1 / T.
Measuring Phase Difference
With both channels displaying, measure the horizontal shift Δt between corresponding points (e.g. both zero-crossings going positive).
Phase difference: φ = (Δt / T) × 360°
If CH2 leads (shifted left), the phase is positive.
Controls
V/div — volts per grid division (per channel). Adjust until trace height is readable.
Time/div — time per grid division (shared timebase). Adjust to show 1–3 complete cycles.
X-Pos — shifts both traces left/right to align a feature (e.g. zero-crossing) with a grid line for easier counting.
CH1 / CH2 buttons — toggle each channel on or off.
Drag the title bar to reposition. Click body to enlarge.

↻ Phasor Diagram

What It Shows
A rotating-vector diagram showing how the voltages (or impedances) in a series circuit relate to each other in magnitude and phase.
The horizontal axis represents the current reference direction. When a resistor is present, Vʀ lies along this axis since current is in phase with Vʀ.
Vʟ (inductor voltage) points upward — it leads the current by 90°.
Vᴄ (capacitor voltage) points downward — it lags the current by 90°.
Vꜱ (supply voltage) is the resultant vector. The angle φ shows the overall phase difference from the current reference. When there is no resistor, a dashed I (ref) arrow shows the current direction.
Circuit Requirements
The phasor diagram is available when the circuit has:
• A signal generator and at least one R, L, or C component in series (any combination works — single component, RL, RC, LC, or full RLC).
• All components connected in series (one single loop). Wires between components are fine.
• No duplicate types — maximum one resistor/bulb, one capacitor, and one inductor.
The checkbox is greyed out if these conditions are not met.
Display Modes
Three radio buttons appear beneath the checkbox:
RMS values — vector labels show RMS voltages (default).
Peak values — vector labels show peak voltages (×√2).
R & X values — vectors represent resistance and reactance in Ω (title changes to Impedance Phasor Diagram).
A badge in the title bar confirms which mode is active.
Diagram Controls
Drag the title bar to reposition the diagram.
Click the diagram body to enlarge it full-size.
Click outside the enlarged diagram to shrink it back.
Animation Mode
In the enlarged view, click the ▶ play button in the title bar to animate the phasors.
All voltage vectors rotate anticlockwise together, maintaining their relative angles — this shows how the frozen phasor diagram is actually a snapshot of continuous rotation.
Sine wave traces appear to the right, with coloured dots that sweep along each wave — connected to the phasor tips by dashed lines, showing how the vertical projection of each rotating phasor generates its sinusoidal waveform.
Click ⏸ pause to freeze at any angle. Click outside to close.

⍸ V-I Waveform Graph

What It Shows
Displays voltage (red) and current (blue) as time-varying sinusoidal waveforms across the selected component(s). This lets you see the CIVIL convention in action — in a Capacitor I leads V, and V leads I in an inductor (L).
How To Use
1. Tick the V-I Waveform checkbox to show the graph.
2. Click any component to select it — the graph shows its V and I waveforms.
3. Click the next adjacent component to extend the selection and see combined voltage.
4. Click a component at either end to remove it from the selection.
Components must form a continuous chain in the same branch.
Graph Controls
Drag the title bar to reposition the small graph.
Click the graph body to enlarge it full-size.
Click outside the enlarged graph to shrink it back.
When enlarged, additional analysis buttons appear in the top-right corner.
I-V Phasor Button
Appears when the graph is enlarged. The button shows a small phasor icon (two arrows with an arc) in the top-right corner. Click it to open a phasor diagram showing the phase relationship between V and I as rotating vectors.
Play/Pause — animates the phasors rotating at angular frequency ω, with sine wave traces drawn alongside.
Works for single or multiple selected components — V shows the combined voltage across the selection and I shows the current through them.
Q Button — Capacitor Charge
Appears when a single capacitor is selected and the graph is enlarged. Click the Q button to open the charge graph.
Shows Q(t) = C × V(t) — how charge on the capacitor varies with time. A gradient marker follows the curve showing the instantaneous slope dQ/dt, which equals the current I.
Where the Q curve is steepest, current is maximum. Where Q is at a peak or trough, current is zero. This visually demonstrates why I leads V by 90° in a capacitor.
L Button — Inductor Voltage
Appears when a single inductor is selected and the graph is enlarged. Click the L button to open the inductor graph.
Shows V = L × dI/dt — a gradient marker follows the current curve showing how its slope determines the voltage. Where current changes fastest (steepest gradient), voltage is at its peak. Where current is at a peak or trough, voltage is zero. This visually demonstrates why V leads I by 90° in an inductor.
P Button — Instantaneous Power
Appears when a single component (resistor, capacitor, or inductor) is selected and the graph is enlarged. Click the P button to open the power graph.

Interactive slider — drag to progressively reveal the power curve P(t) = V × I. As you sweep across time you see:
• A red dot on V, a blue dot on I, and a gold dot on P, each showing their instantaneous value.
• Green shading where P > 0 (energy being stored or dissipated).
• Red shading where P < 0 (energy being returned to the supply).

For capacitors and inductors: green and red areas cancel every half-cycle, giving Mean Power = 0. An energy bar on the right shows the energy stored (½CV² or ½LI²) rising and falling — ending exactly where it started after one full cycle.

For resistors: power is always positive (all green shading). There is no energy bar because energy is dissipated as heat, not stored. The mean power line shows the actual power consumed.

Component Labels
Each component type has a dropdown with three display modes:
Value — shows the actual value (e.g. 100.0 Ω, 46.5 µF)
Symbol — shows a symbolic label (e.g. R₁, C₁, L₁)
Hide — hides the label completely
Labels can be dragged to reposition them on the canvas
Hide Component
Click a resistor, capacitor, or inductor and untick Show component type in the info panel
The component is replaced by a mystery box labelled with a letter (A, B, C…)
Use the oscilloscope or V-I waveform to identify it — clicking the box will not reveal it while these tools are active
Click the mystery box to reveal the component when the tools are off

Inductor: current lags voltage by up to 90°
